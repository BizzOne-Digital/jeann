# Buyer transaction process: client-approved specification

Source: client's own marked-up diagram (received 2026-11-13). This is the **right process** according to the client.
It applies to the **Buyer, Broker and Agent portals**. The **Supplier portal is different** and will be sent separately.

Every step is a back-and-forth between the Buyer portal and Admin (Finekarts).
Every step on both sides must allow upload, download and signing of documents.
On the Admin side, staff must be able to update and correct any document Finekarts generated, and must have access to the fully formatted version of every generated document.

## Starting point (confirmed by the client on 2026-11-13)

The process starts when Finekarts sends the buyer an **SCO or FCO**: the product offer with price per MT and Incoterms.
This is Finekarts' offer to the buyer. The buyer **accepts it by submitting the ICPO**. That is why the ICPO is irrevocable.
From there the parties proceed to the contract (the PSA).
So the ICPO should be based on the SCO/FCO the buyer received (pre-filled where possible), not a blank form.

## The 7 steps

| # | Admin (Finekarts) | Buyer portal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Generate a pre-loaded SCO or FCO with the product name and price (FOB and CIF). Send it to the buyer. | Buyer receives and reviews the SCO/FCO. Buyer submits a generated or pre-loaded ICPO or LOI, or uploads their own. |
| 2 | Receive and review the ICPO or LOI. Request CIS and RWA. Staff may make an exception for the FIS (see rules). The buyer may also upload ICPO/LOI, CIS, RWA and FIS all at once. | If the buyer did not upload RWA, CIS and FIS in step 1, the buyer sends them in this step. The RWA is used only for LC transactions. |
| 3 | Receive and review CIS, RWA, FIS. Generate the PSA automatically from the ICPO or LOI (must meet bank regulation). Send the PSA with the LC wording to the buyer to sign. | Buyer receives, reviews, signs and stamps the PSA. Attach a copy of the passport of the person in charge (not employees). Send to Finekarts. |
| 4 | Receive the signed and stamped PSA with the buyer's passport. Finekarts signs and stamps the PSA and attaches a copy of the passport of the person in charge (manager, president or owner, not employees). | Buyer receives a copy of the fully signed PSA. After the contract is signed, Finekarts also sends the Proforma Invoice (PI). |
| 5 | Generate a banking-regulated Proforma Invoice. Request the Letter of Credit type and exact amount agreed on the PSA. Everything must match. | Buyer receives the PI and creates the exact LC type agreed on the PSA. **The buyer must submit the LC from the issuing bank within 24 hours.** |
| 6 | Receive the LC. Send all contract paperwork to the banking advisor for review and approval. The bank opens an LC account to buy from the supplier. | If the buyer issued a faulty LC or one that does not match the PSA, the buyer pays the amendment on both banks. If the LC is good, there is no amendment to pay. |
| 7 | After collecting all LC documents and certificates agreed on the PSA, the advisor reviews all documents and sends them to the buyer's bank for payment. | The seller and bank submit all documents to the buyer's bank. The buyer's bank receives all shipping documents and sends the final payment via SWIFT MT103. |

## Rules

- Every month the issuer's bank must [handle/renew] the LC at the exact dates, same amount, same LC types. (Wording unclear in the diagram; confirm with the client.)
- The RWA is required on every LC transaction, regardless of small or large.
- A returning buyer's CIS and FIS are already on record. A new buyer uploads the RWA and the CIS.
- The FIS: Finekarts can make an exception depending on the situation. In large LC transactions the FIS is definitely required.

## Differences from what was built before this spec (to fix)

1. Step 1 starts with Finekarts sending an SCO/FCO to the buyer, then the buyer submits ICPO/LOI. The buyer-only tabs started at the ICPO.
2. The deadline to submit the LC is **24 hours**, not 72 hours.
3. The FIS is required by default. Staff can make an exception. It is always required for large LC transactions. (An optional-FIS rule was built earlier; that was based on an earlier message and is superseded.)
4. The PSA is generated by Finekarts from the ICPO/LOI and sent with the LC wording. Finekarts also attaches the passport of the person in charge when it counter-signs.
5. Step 6 covers faulty LC and amendment cost; step 7 covers the shipping documents and MT103 payment. Neither exists in the portal today.
6. Admin needs one place per transaction that shows these steps with download, upload, sign and edit-generated-document at each step.

## Flexibility, regulation and pre-filled documents (client, 2026-11-15)

- **Supplier portal:** everything starts from the supplier.
- **The process is not static.** Some trades are discussed by text/chat and have no SCO or FCO. Many companies do not follow the process the way they should. The portals must therefore allow a trade to start at a later step (for example an ICPO or LOI with no SCO/FCO) and let authorized staff skip or reorder steps with a recorded reason.
- **Regulation:** Finekarts follows ICC commodity trade regulations.
- **Most important requirement:** every document is generated **pre-filled** for each product. Fields to fill automatically: product name, company name, loading port, discharging port, Incoterms, price and specification. Staff can generate the documents in a few clicks.

## How a trade starts, and payment terms (client, 2026-11-16)

- The SCO/FCO is used to **make an offer**. If it is not ready, the trade is discussed by text, email or phone. When both sides agree on terms, the trade proceeds.
- **Price and payment terms are the key part of the discussion.**
- Finekarts works with **TT** and **Irrevocable Transferable Letter of Credit**. The buyer may have different terms. (See the 2026-11-18 correction below on how these relate.)
- So staff must be able to open a transaction directly from terms agreed outside the portal (no SCO/FCO, no portal request), entering price and payment terms.
- (Superseded by the 2026-11-18 correction below: the CIS, RWA and FIS are verification documents and are not tied to the payment term.)

## Correction: CIS, RWA, FIS and payment instruments (client, 2026-11-18)

- **The Irrevocable Transferable LC is one of many banking instruments** that can be used in trades. It is not the only one.
- **TT (Telegraphic Transfer) is another way to pay, but it is unsafe for the buyer.**
- **CIS, RWA and FIS are not related to payment terms.** They are verification documents:
  - **RWA** (ready, willing and able): the supplier requests it to make sure the buyer has the funds ready for the transaction.
  - **CIS**: verifies the corporation's registration details.
  - **FIS**: helps verify the banking institutions that handle the transaction.
  - Together they check that the buyer is legitimate.
- If the FIS is not collected at the beginning, there are ways to find out where that bank ranks among the **top 50 banks**.

**Open conflict to confirm with the client:** the diagrams say "RWA only used in LC transactions" and "RWA is required on every LC transaction", while this message says the documents are not related to payment terms. The current code only requires the RWA/CIS for LC payment terms. Need to confirm whether TT trades also request CIS, RWA and FIS.

**TT (client, 2026-11-19):** TT is **not a banking instrument**, and not many buyers use it. Keep TT out of the banking-instrument list. Treat TT as a minor, rarely used payment method; the main flow is built around banking instruments such as the Irrevocable Transferable LC.

**Answer on TT and the verification documents (client, 2026-11-20), resolves the open conflict above:**
- If a buyer agrees to pay by **TT, no RWA is necessary**.
- The buyer should still provide the **CIS and FIS for verification**, on every trade.
- So: the **RWA** depends on the payment (needed for LC and other banking instruments, not for TT). The **CIS** and **FIS** are requested on every trade (FIS keeps the exception rules recorded earlier).
- Current code only requires RWA and CIS when the payment term is an LC (`src/lib/transactions/banking-service.ts`, `src/lib/banking/rwa.ts`). CIS/FIS are not requested on TT trades, and the CIS/RWA/FIS tab always shows the RWA row.

## Simplification, payment terms and order numbers (client, 2026-11-21)

**CIS, RWA, FIS: keep them simple.**
- Finekarts requests them in the SCO/FCO, or by whatever means the trade is discussed. The buyer uploads them. Finekarts verifies them. That is all.
- Do not tie them to payment terms or to regulation: "there is no regulation for that". (This simplifies the earlier LC/TT rules; the automatic blocking in `banking-service.ts` goes beyond what the client asked for.)
- General rule: **an upload button and a download button at every step.**

**Payment terms.**
- Only a **manager** can change the payment terms.
- If the buyer has different terms, that is a matter of discussion until both sides agree.
- When Finekarts buys from a supplier, Finekarts chooses the payment terms.

**Supplier order must match the buyer order.**
- Product name, quantity, quality, destination port and Incoterm on the supplier order must be **exactly the same** as the buyer order Finekarts is buying for.
- Example: a buyer buys 500 MT of sugar. The sale is completed on the buyer portal. The **same order number is used in the supplier portal, with a modification** to tell the two apart.
- Current code: buyer orders are numbered `FK-S-YYYY-NNNNNN` and supplier orders `FK-P-YYYY-NNNNNN` from two separate counters, so they are unrelated. `Transaction.linkedTransactionId` exists but is not used to derive the number or lock the fields.
- Current code: any staff user with `transactions:write` can change payment terms (`/api/transactions/[id]/commercial-terms`). The manager-level permission is `transactions:approve`.

**Back-to-back (client, 2026-11-15):** the products Finekarts sells, Finekarts buys in the **same product, with the same terms, everything the same**. The supplier order is a mirror of the buyer order: product, quantity, quality, destination port, Incoterm and the terms.

## Contract and Proforma: the exact order, in the client's own words (2026-11-27). Follow this, do not reinterpret it.

1. Finekarts sends the buyer the **PSA and the LC wording**.
2. The buyer **signs and stamps the PSA, attaches the passport of the director, and sends it back**.
3. Finekarts **signs and stamps it and attaches its own passport**. At this point both parties have signed and the PSA **becomes a contract**.
4. Finekarts then **sends a copy of the contract (the signed PSA) to the buyer together with the Proforma Invoice**.
5. The buyer uses the Proforma Invoice and the PSA to open the LC (deadline agreed on the PSA, negotiable, hours or business hours).

So the Proforma Invoice is NOT sent with the first PSA. It goes out with the copy of the signed contract.
(2026-11-26 an earlier message was read as "the Proforma goes with the PSA and the LC wording" and the code was changed; that was wrong and was reverted. Before changing the process from a message that seems to disagree with this file or the diagram, ask the client what he means.)

Nothing is final until the end of the trade: the LC deadline can be changed, an amended PSA can be issued and signed again after signing, and banking errors lead to amendments. Forms stay flexible.

## LC deadline default (client, 2026-11-27)

## Second stage: trade documents and certificates (client, 2026-11-27)
- Stage 1 ends with the Proforma Invoice (sent with the copy of the signed contract).
- Stage 2 is the trade documents and certificates that Finekarts agreed in the PSA to provide to the buyer (COO, HACCP, Kosher, Halal, shipping documents, and the documents the buyer needs at the port of discharge). If Finekarts agreed to provide them, it must.
- Finekarts buys the exact same product from its supplier and signs a contract with the supplier to provide the product, the shipping documents and the certificates.
- **Requirements vary from port to port and regulations change at any time.** Example: Canada changed its labelling rules last year, and a shipment sent with the old label is arrested. So the list of required documents must never be fixed in code: staff must be able to add document types and change the requirements for a port or country whenever the rules change, and change the list of a single transaction at any time.

The document TYPES are a fixed list in `src/lib/shipments/checklist-service.ts`, so a new type needs a developer (only "Other" is free). Checklists are built by hand per shipment lot and are not taken from the PSA. Not yet built; waiting for the client to confirm the plan.

### Correction on stage 2 (client, 2026-11-27, same day): documents are managed per transaction
- The certificates and documents are **part of what the buyer requests**. Finekarts does not know in advance what the buyer will buy, so it cannot know the documents in advance.
- Therefore the documents are **managed inside each transaction**, by the people running that transaction. **The platform must not dictate how Finekarts conducts the transaction.**
- Do NOT build mandatory per-port lists or a fixed catalogue that forces documents on a transaction. Per-port lists, if ever added, are only optional helpers that staff may ignore. Document names must be free text (any certificate, any label requirement), not limited to a fixed list.
- Each transaction has its own list of agreed documents that staff can add to, change and remove at any time, with a status per item (agreed, received from the supplier, provided to the buyer). The earlier idea of per-port lists that fill in new transactions is withdrawn until the client asks for it.

## FINAL INSTRUCTIONS (client, 2026-11-28). This replaces every earlier step list. Read it and follow it exactly.

Each transaction has TWO STAGES. It starts in the BUYER portal, where Finekarts is the SUPPLIER (STAGE 1), and it ends in the SUPPLIER portal, where Finekarts is the BUYER (STAGE 2). Stage 2 completes the purchase order of stage 1. On the platform this is called the **workflow** (in the SCO/FCO document the same thing is called the **trade procedure**, so the buyer already knows every step).

The transaction number of stage 1 must match stage 2, with a few letters added to tell them apart (the supplier order keeps the buyer order number with a suffix).

### STAGE 1: buyer portal (Finekarts is the supplier), 8 steps
1. Finekarts issues the SCO or FCO (the offer). The document carries the trade procedure, identical to the workflow, explaining every step to the buyer.
2. The buyer reviews and accepts the offer and submits the ICPO (a purchase order that is irrevocable).
3. Finekarts formally requests verification documents (the buyer already knows after reading the SCO/FCO). The buyer uploads CIS, RWA and FIS.
4. Finekarts reviews CIS, RWA and FIS. If satisfied, Finekarts uses the buyer's ICPO to prepare the PSA.
5. Finekarts sends the PSA together with the LC wording to the buyer for review and approval. If the buyer is satisfied (it matches the purchase order) the buyer signs, stamps, attaches a copy of the passport and sends it back. If there are discrepancies the buyer requests changes or an amendment.
6. Finekarts receives the signed PSA, signs it, stamps it and attaches a copy of the director's passport. It is now a full contract.
7. The PSA is signed by both parties and is a contract. Finekarts sends a final copy of the contract (PSA) to the buyer together with the Proforma Invoice, for the buyer to open the LC account within 48 business hours.
8. Space for amendments; there can be several. The buyer's inspection request is granted at the loading port; the report is provided in stage 2.
End of stage 1, proceed to stage 2.

### STAGE 2: supplier portal (Finekarts is the buyer), 9 steps
1. Finekarts receives an SCO/FCO from the supplier, or discusses the opportunity by chat, instant message, email or WhatsApp, and decides whether to proceed.
2. Finekarts submits its ICPO to the supplier (a purchase order that completes the purchase order of stage 1). It must be the buyer's ICPO; only the company name is different.
3. When the supplier asks for verification documents, Finekarts uploads CIS, RWA and FIS (the supplier may ask for others too).
4. The supplier prepares the PSA using the terms, conditions and product specification of the purchase order, with the LC wording, and sends it to Finekarts.
5. Finekarts reviews the PSA, signs it, stamps it, attaches a copy of the director's passport and returns it to the supplier.
6. The supplier signs, stamps and attaches a copy of the director's passport: the contract is complete on the supplier's side. The supplier sends the contract to Finekarts together with a Proforma Invoice.
7. Finekarts receives the full contract, checks it matches the purchase order (no amendment needed), and takes all the paperwork and the Proforma Invoice to its bank to create the LC account.
8. Finekarts sends the LC to the supplier as agreed on the PSA. The supplier then provides all the trade documents requested by the buyer's ICPO in stage 1.
9. Finekarts collects all documents from the supplier, with its own due diligence, the inspection report, the verification report, all certificates, the Bill of Lading and every document of the transaction, with the commercial invoice, and sends them to the banking advisor, who reviews the contract (PSA) and every document and checks they are authentic. Then OUR BANK SENDS THE CONTRACT WITH ALL DOCUMENTS REQUESTED BY THE BUYER ON THE PURCHASE ORDER TO THE BUYER'S BANK. The buyer's bank reviews the contract and all documents (shipping documents, certificates, BL, inspection report); they must match the pre-order inspection (POI). When everything matches, the buyer's bank completes the transaction: the final payment of the total contract amount (PSA of stage 1) by MT103.

### Rules that apply to both stages
- The whole process is time sensitive: everything is done within the agreed timeframe. The contract is filled once, but the documents process repeats monthly for the duration of the contract.
- Our bank deducts the LC fees and banking fees; if there is an amendment the bank charges an amendment fee. MT101 is deposited to Finekarts' corporate bank account. Finekarts pays the supplier, which may happen beforehand.
- How to work with the client: just read and follow the steps and adjust the workflow. Do NOT send long messages with many questions. If a question is really needed it must be specific and name the stage and the step.
